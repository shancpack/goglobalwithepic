FBA, FBM, or both? Using a 3PL around Amazon's 2026 fees

Amazon's 2026 fee changes reward sellers who keep FBA stocked lean and steady. Here's how a 3PL fits around that, as a buffer for FBA and as the engine for FBM.

Epic Warehouse Operations · November 4, 2026 · 7 min read

The short version

  • Amazon's 2026 changes penalize both too little inventory (low-inventory fees) and too much (aged-inventory surcharges starting earlier).
  • A 3PL lets you hold bulk stock cheaply off Amazon and feed FBA in right-sized case and pallet shipments.
  • FBM through a 3PL makes sense for slow movers, oversized items and anything you also sell on Shopify.

For years the default advice for Amazon sellers was simple: send everything to FBA. That is getting harder to justify. Amazon's fees now push sellers toward holding a steady, moderate amount of stock in its network. It doesn't want too little, and it doesn't want too much. That is exactly the gap a third-party warehouse fills.

What changed in 2026

Amazon announces fee changes in Seller Central, and the details vary by size tier and category. Always confirm current numbers there. Industry coverage of the 2026 changes highlighted several shifts:1,2

  • Low-inventory-level fees are measured per FNSKU instead of per parent ASIN, and apply to a variant until it's restocked above 28 days of supply, according to eFulfillment Service's summary.1 One under-stocked color or size can now trigger the fee on its own.
  • Aged-inventory surcharges start earlier, at 181 days instead of 271.1
  • Inbound placement fees still apply when you choose to ship to fewer Amazon locations. Splitting shipments across more fulfillment centers can bring the fee to zero, but every split is another shipment to build.1,3
  • A 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge on FBA fulfillment fees in the US and Canada took effect April 17, 2026, as reported.1
  • Q4 storage rates continue to climb sharply from October through December.
The 2026 pattern: Amazon wants your inventory in its network in steady, predictable amounts. Overstocking costs you, and so does running thin.

Strategy 1: Use a 3PL as your FBA buffer

Instead of shipping a full production run straight into FBA, land it at a 3PL and replenish Amazon in smaller waves. You get:

  • Cheaper long-term storage. A pallet position at Epic is $17 a month, with no Q4 multiplier on our rate card.
  • Fewer aged-inventory surcharges, because only a few weeks of supply sits at Amazon at any time.
  • Protection against low-inventory fees, because the next case shipment is days away instead of a production cycle away.
  • Room to split shipments across Amazon locations to reduce placement fees, without asking your factory to do it.

In practice the outbound side is case and pallet orders: $4 a case, $20 for a single-SKU pallet, $30 for mixed. FNSKU labeling is prep and billed at $35 an hour, so ask your manufacturer to pre-label if you can. See Example 2 in our cost breakdowns for a sample month.

Strategy 2: Run FBM from the same inventory

Fulfilled-by-merchant orders go straight from the 3PL to the customer. That works well for:

  • Slow movers and long-tail variants that would age out in FBA.
  • Oversized or heavy items where FBA's bulky-tier fees eat the margin.
  • Backup coverage when an FBA shipment is stuck in receiving or a variant runs low.
  • One pool of inventory shared with your Shopify store, so you aren't splitting stock across channels.

At Epic, FBM and Shopify orders are billed the same way: $1.70 per order with the first unit included, plus $0.30 per additional unit, with shipping and packaging on top. Orders sync through ShipHero, so there's no order-entry fee.

When straight FBA still wins

A hybrid isn't for everyone. If you sell a small number of fast-moving, small-standard SKUs with steady demand and can forecast replenishment precisely, sending directly to FBA can be the simplest answer. The hybrid model earns its keep when you have many variants, lumpy demand, large production runs, oversized products or multiple sales channels.

A quick decision checklist

  1. How many FNSKUs do you have, and how many dip below four weeks of supply each quarter?
  2. How much of your FBA inventory is older than 180 days?
  3. Do you receive production in runs larger than two or three months of sales?
  4. Do you sell the same SKUs on Shopify, Walmart or wholesale?

If you answered "a lot" or "yes" to two or more, a 3PL buffer will likely pay for itself. Send us your SKU list and we'll model it.
