What a month at a 3PL actually costs: 5 worked examples

A Shopify brand, an Amazon seller restocking FBA, a wholesale account and two inbound scenarios, priced line by line on Epic's published rate card.

Epic Warehouse Operations · November 4, 2026 · 6 min read

A rate card is only useful once you run your own numbers through it. Here are five realistic scenarios priced on Epic's published 3PL rates. Shipping postage and packaging materials are left out on purpose, since they depend on your carrier accounts, box sizes and destinations.

Example 1 · D2C brand

A Shopify brand shipping 1,000 orders a month

Average order is 1.5 units, so 500 additional units a month. Orders flow in through the ShipHero integration. Inventory sits on 6 pallets plus 40 bin locations for fast movers.

1,000 orders × $1.70 (first unit included)$1,700.00
500 additional units × $0.30$150.00
Order entry via API$0.00
6 pallet positions × $17$102.00
40 bin locations × $5$200.00
Monthly warehouse cost$2,152.00

That works out to about $2.15 per order before postage and boxes. If the same brand emailed its orders instead, entry would add $2.00 × 1,000 = $2,000 a month, nearly doubling the bill. Connect the integration.

Example 2 · Amazon seller

Feeding FBA from a 3PL

The brand keeps bulk stock at Epic and sends Amazon what it needs every month: two single-SKU pallets plus 10 single cases to top up slower variants. The cases need Amazon FNSKU labels, which takes about two hours.

2 single-SKU pallet orders × $20$40.00
2 Epic wood pallets × $12$24.00
10 case orders × $4$40.00
2 hours of labeling × $35$70.00
Outbound to Amazon$174.00

Labels are prep, so they're billed as labor and not included in case or pallet rates. Having your manufacturer pre-label units removes that line entirely. For why this split is worth considering in 2026, see FBA, FBM, or both?

Example 3 · Wholesale

A mixed pallet for a retail account

A boutique chain orders 14 cases across six SKUs. Epic builds, wraps and labels one mixed pallet on an Epic wood pallet.

1 mixed-SKU pallet order$30.00
Wood pallet from Epic$12.00
Order cost$42.00

Shipped as 14 separate case orders it would be 14 × $4 = $56. A useful rule of thumb from the rate card: a $20 single-SKU pallet order costs the same as five $4 case orders (or eight, if you add Epic's $12 wood pallet).

Example 4 · Inbound

Palletized vs. loose cartons

The factory ships 400 cartons of a single SKU. Option A: 10 single-SKU pallets of 40 cartons each. Option B: the same cartons arrive loose.

Option A: 10 single-SKU pallets × $12$120.00
Option B: 400 single-SKU boxes × $5$2,000.00
Difference$1,880.00

Once a single-SKU pallet holds three or more cartons, receiving it as a pallet costs less than receiving its cartons one by one. Ask your supplier to palletize by SKU.

Example 5 · Storage

The 72-inch pallet

A supplier stacks pallets to 72 inches to save freight. Epic's standard is 60 inches, so each one is reconfigured into two pallets.

Expected: 1 position × $17$17.00 / mo
Actual: 2 positions × $17$34.00 / mo

Across 20 pallets that's $340 a month in extra storage. Building to 60 inches at origin is free.

What these examples have in common

  • Automation pays. API orders carry no entry fee. Emailed orders cost $2 each.
  • Single-SKU beats mixed on both receiving ($12 vs. $18 a pallet) and outbound ($20 vs. $30).
  • Prep is labor. Labels, bundles and kits are $35 an hour, so push them upstream to your manufacturer when you can.
  • Pallet height is a storage decision. Stay at or under 60 inches.
