How to send inventory to a 3PL without receiving delays

ASNs, labels, pallet builds and the 60-inch rule. A practical checklist for getting product from your factory onto our shelves quickly, accurately and at the lowest receiving rate.

Epic Warehouse Operations · November 4, 2026 · 6 min read

Receiving is where most first-month frustrations with a 3PL begin. A shipment shows up unannounced, cartons aren't labeled, three SKUs are stacked on one pallet, and suddenly inventory that's physically in the building isn't available to sell for days. Nearly all of it is avoidable with a little prep upstream.

Step 1: Send an ASN before anything ships

An advance ship notice (ASN) tells the warehouse what's coming: which SKUs, how many, how they're packed and roughly when. It lets the receiving team plan dock time and check the count against an expected list instead of guessing.1,2

At Epic: create the ASN in your ShipHero portal before the shipment leaves, listing the expected SKUs and quantities. Give us notice before any inbound arrives.

A good ASN matches what's physically on the truck. Under-reported or over-reported counts are the most common reason a receipt gets held for review.

Step 2: Label every carton and pallet

Receiving best practices are remarkably consistent across the industry:3

  • Every carton should show the SKU, the quantity inside, and a scannable barcode.
  • Every pallet should carry a label with the PO or ASN reference, the SKU (or "MIXED"), carton count and units per carton.
  • Put labels in the same place on every pallet, on at least one side, and on top of the stretch wrap rather than under it, where scanners can't read them.
  • Print clearly. Faded or low-contrast barcodes force manual entry, which is slower and less accurate.

Step 3: Build pallets by SKU

Single-SKU pallets are faster to count, faster to put away, and cheaper to receive. On Epic's rate card a single-SKU pallet is $12 to receive and a mixed-SKU pallet is $18. If a mixed pallet is unavoidable, group each SKU together in its own layer or column and label it.

Palletize whenever you can. Receiving loose cartons costs $5 per single-SKU box, so a single-SKU pallet of three or more cartons is already cheaper than receiving those cartons individually. For a full container the difference adds up fast. We priced it out in Example 4.

Step 4: Keep pallets at or under 60 inches

Standard racking has a height limit. At Epic, any pallet taller than 60 inches is reconfigured into two pallets and charged as two pallet positions for storage. Tell your freight forwarder and factory before they build, because the fix is free at origin and costs money at the warehouse.

  • Use standard 48" × 40" pallets in good condition.
  • Don't let cartons overhang the edges.
  • Put heavy cartons on the bottom, and stretch-wrap tightly.

Step 5: Think about prep before it ships

If your units need Amazon FNSKU labels, polybags, bundles or kits, the cheapest place to do it is usually the factory. At a 3PL that work is labor. At Epic it's $35 an hour. Sometimes it's still the right call, for example when you want flexibility to label only what's going to Amazon. Just make it a deliberate decision.

Inbound checklist

  1. ASN created in ShipHero with SKUs and quantities.
  2. Cartons labeled with SKU, quantity and barcode.
  3. Pallets built by SKU, labeled on the outside of the wrap.
  4. Pallets 60 inches tall or less, on sound 48" × 40" pallets.
  5. Prep (labels, bundles, kits) done at origin, or scheduled with us.
  6. Carrier and ETA shared so we can plan dock time.
