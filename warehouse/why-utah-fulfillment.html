Why ship from Utah? The case for a Mountain West warehouse

Interstates 15 and 80, a deep logistics workforce, and ground reach to most of the country in two days. Why brands are adding Utah to their fulfillment map.

Epic Warehouse Operations · November 4, 2026 · 5 min read

When brands pick a first warehouse, they often default to wherever their freight lands: the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach, or New Jersey. Those are expensive places to store inventory, and they sit at the edges of the country. A growing number of brands are choosing the middle of the West instead.

Reach: most of the country within two days by ground

Northern Utah sits at the junction of Interstate 15 (north-south, from Southern California to Montana) and Interstate 80 (east-west, from the Bay Area toward Chicago and New York). Retail Insider, citing industry data, reports that roughly 96 percent of the U.S. population can be reached within two days by ground from Salt Lake City, and that Los Angeles, Las Vegas, Phoenix and Denver are all within one to two days.1

Ground service is where the savings are. Shipping from a central western location shortens the zones to Mountain West and Midwest customers compared with fulfilling everything from a coast.1

Where we are: Epic's warehouse is in Springville, Utah, right off I-15 about an hour south of Salt Lake City. 530 S 2000 W, Suite C (Northwest door).

A logistics economy, not just a location

Utah has built distribution into its economy on purpose. Retail Insider reports that logistics-reliant industries contribute about $78.2 billion a year, around 37 percent of state output, and support roughly 547,000 jobs. The state's Inland Port Authority has also launched more than a dozen project areas to expand freight infrastructure.1 For a 3PL client, that means experienced labor, strong carrier coverage and a mature freight network for inbound containers and LTL.

Where a Utah node fits in your network

  • As your only warehouse, if most of your customers are west of the Mississippi or you're optimizing for ground shipping costs over overnight speed.
  • As a second node alongside an East Coast warehouse, splitting the country for faster delivery on both sides.
  • As an Amazon buffer close to several western Amazon fulfillment centers, feeding FBA in cases and pallets. More on that in FBA, FBM, or both?
  • As a landing point for West Coast imports, moving inventory inland where storage is cheaper.

Questions to ask about any location

  1. Where are my customers, by state or zip3, over the last 12 months?
  2. What share of my orders ship ground vs. expedited?
  3. Where does my freight land, and what does it cost to move it inland?
  4. Which channels need what delivery promise (Prime, Shopify, wholesale)?
